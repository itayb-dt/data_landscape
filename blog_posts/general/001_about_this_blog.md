Welcome to the first post in my "Data Landscape" series, where I'll be sharing insights from my journey as a founder, CTO, and product leader in the data ecosystem.







The data industry has undergone seismic shifts over the past decade. Having lived through these changes as a founder of two acquired startups (X2 and SkyGiraffe, acquired by ServiceNow, and Metis, acquired by Dynatrace) and later as a principal product manager working on data products in large enterprises, I've witnessed firsthand how organizations struggle—and sometimes succeed—in building strategic data capabilities.

Why This Series Matters

Most discussions about data focus on the tactical: which tools to use, how to optimize pipelines, or the latest machine learning frameworks. While these topics have their place, they miss the bigger picture. The real challenge isn't technical, it's strategic. How do you build data systems that survive organizational changes, market shifts, and the relentless pace of technological evolution?

This series aims to bridge that gap by providing a high-level, strategic understanding of the data landscape from someone who has been both a builder and an acquirer, both a startup founder and a corporate product leader.

What We'll Cover

Over the coming posts, I'll dive deep into several key areas:

The Acquisition Landscape: We'll examine the recent data startup acquisitions: who's buying whom, why, and what it signals about market direction.

Investment Patterns and Thesis Evolution: VCs are placing massive bets on data infrastructure, but their thesis has evolved significantly. We'll analyze where the smart money is going and why certain categories are attracting disproportionate investment while others are being written off.

The AI Transformation: The generative AI boom isn't just changing how we build applications, it's fundamentally reshaping data architecture, governance, and strategy. We'll explore how forward-thinking organizations are adapting their data strategies to thrive in an AI-first world.

Corporate vs. Startup Perspectives: Having operated on both sides of the acquisition table, I'll share insights into how large enterprises and scrappy startups approach data differently and why understanding both perspectives is crucial for success.

Where We're Heading: The data industry is at an inflection point. Cloud adoption is mature, AI is mainstream, and new challenges around privacy, cost, and complexity are emerging. We'll explore what the next decade might hold.

Lessons from the Journey

My perspective comes from a diverse journey through the data ecosystem: solution architect at Microsoft UK, co-founder and CTO of a database-focused professional services company, CTO of a startup acquired by ServiceNow, Senior Director at ServiceNow, then a new startup acquired by Dynatrace. Now, as a principal product manager, I see how enterprise organizations actually consume and scale data solutions.

Here's what I've learned: tactical excellence is table stakes. The organizations that will thrive think strategically about data as a competitive advantage, not just a technical necessity. Data architecture decisions made today echo through businesses for years to come.

I believe you learn the most from your mistakes, and I've had plenty to learn from over 25 years in this industry.

On Timing and Perspective

This blog focuses on insights that require time to process and understand. I typically wait 1-3 months after an acquisition or product announcement before writing about it, enough time to separate signal from noise and determine if the subject truly warrants attention. There are so many distractions, hype, and noise that it's hard to focus on what's important. In a world of instant hot takes, I believe the most valuable perspectives come from letting events settle and patterns emerge.
